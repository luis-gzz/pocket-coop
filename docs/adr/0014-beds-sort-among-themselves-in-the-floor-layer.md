# Hen beds get their own sorted floor layer, and a minimum-separation rule

ADR-0005/0006 gave every world object a place in one shared depth sort. Hen beds were left out of it entirely instead — they're flat and meant to always render behind the chicken and every other object, so giving them a depth value in the shared sort would have been meaningless. That worked as long as beds never visually overlapped each other, but nothing stopped two beds from being dragged fully on top of one another, and once that's possible, whichever bed happens to be a later sibling in the display tree wins arbitrarily.

We're keeping beds out of the main sort (still always behind everything else), but giving them their own floor layer that sorts beds against each other by the same bottom-edge depth rule ADR-0006 established, so an overlapping pair still draws in a sensible order. Alongside that, placing or dragging a bed is now rejected if it would land within half a bed-width *and* half a bed-height of another bed's center simultaneously — instead, it's pushed out just far enough on one axis to clear that zone. We considered just forbidding any overlap at all (like food items' jitter-away behavior), but rejected it: beds are decorative floor objects a player might reasonably want to cluster tightly, and the "not directly on top of each other" rule is enough to keep the sort meaningful without being as restrictive as food placement.
