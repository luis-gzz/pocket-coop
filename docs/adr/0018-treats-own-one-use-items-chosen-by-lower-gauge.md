# Treats owns every one-use item, chosen by lower gauge and seen through once claimed

Adding ash, a one-use item that isn't food, split ADR-0011's Feed in two along how an item is used up rather than what it does for the chicken: Feed keeps the food sources (shared pools many chickens eat from), and a new sibling module, Treats, owns every item used up whole by one claiming chicken - the mealworm moved there alongside ash. "Treat" therefore no longer implies food, and "food item" is retired. The alternatives were a separate Pamper owner for non-food items only (leaving the mealworm in Feed and two modules running the same alert/claim logic) or keeping everything in Feed (making it no longer purely "what there is to eat").

Two rules that are easy to "fix" back without this context. When several treats are in alert range, a chicken prefers the one that helps its lower gauge (satiety vs cleanliness), then the nearest - not simply the nearest, so a hungry chicken isn't pulled into a bath. And once claimed, a treat is seen through: no newer or nearer treat steals the chicken and a pending lay waits, because the per-frame alert check would otherwise make a chicken flip-flop between items (or abandon a bath mid-shimmy) as new ones are placed. Only being picked up, or the player dragging the treat away, breaks a claim.

The happiness buff every treat grants now lasts an hour and restarts on a repeat grant. Offline catch-up still ignores it (ADR-0016 called it short-lived), which is deliberate: at most about an hour of slightly faster laying is lost, not worth another catch-up phase.
